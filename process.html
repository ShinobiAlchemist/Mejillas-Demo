Our story · Chapter two

From red clay to your glass

Nothing in this process is fast, and nothing in it is secret. Here is the whole road.

  1. Harvest

    The jimador reads each plant — not a calendar. Only hand-selected mature agave makes the cut: the coa strips the leaves and frees the piña at its sweetest.

  2. Slow-roast

    Whole piñas bake for about 36 hours in brick ovens. Slow steam turns starch to sugar without scorching a single note.

  3. Ferment

    The pressed juice ferments naturally, unhurried, in open tanks. This is where aroma is born, so we give it all the days it asks for.

  4. Distill

    Two passes through small copper pot stills. We keep only the heart of the run — the rest never sees a bottle.

  5. Rest

    Blanco rests briefly in steel. Reposado takes months in American oak; the Extra Añejo waits years in select barrels.

  6. Bottle

    Small batches, hand-checked, NOM and distillery printed on every label. Then it is your turn.

Our mezcal takes a different road — crafted in Oaxaca, its agave roasted underground the traditional way, which is where the gentle smoke comes from. Compare the spirits.
