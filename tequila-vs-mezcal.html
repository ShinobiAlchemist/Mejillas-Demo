Our story · Chapter three

Tequila vs mezcal, settled in five minutes

They are cousins, not twins. Both are agave spirits — but the plant, the place, and the fire are different, and you can taste all three.

The short version

Tequila must be made from one plant — blue Weber agave — grown in Jalisco and a handful of neighboring regions, and its piñas are usually steam-cooked in ovens. The result is bright, peppery, and fruit-forward.

Mezcal can be made from dozens of agave species under its own denomination of origin, and its piñas are traditionally roasted in earthen pits over hot stone. That fire is the signature: earthy depth and a ribbon of smoke.

So: mezcal is never tequila, and tequila is never mezcal. They are separate spirits with separate rules, and each deserves its own name on the label.

Side-by-side comparison of tequila and mezcal
 TequilaMezcal
AgaveBlue Weber onlyDozens of species; espadín is most common
WhereJalisco and limited neighboring regionsIts own multi-state denomination of origin
CookingSteam — brick ovens or autoclavesEarthen pits over hot stone
FlavorBright, peppery, cooked-agave sweetEarthy, roasted, gently smoky
RulesIts own official standard and certifying councilA separate standard and council of its own

Where Mejillas fits

Our Blanco, Reposado, and Extra Añejo are 100% blue Weber highland tequilas. Our Mezcal is a separate line — pit-roasted espadín, labeled and certified as mezcal, never as tequila. Same table, different chairs.
